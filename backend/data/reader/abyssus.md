# The Grove of the Well

The stones here are ancient. Long before the first settlers carved their names into the valley walls, this grove stood — patient, watching, indifferent to the turning of seasons and the short lives of those who passed beneath its canopy.

You follow the worn path downhill. The roots of enormous oaks have long since swallowed what was once a cobbled road. Their bark is dark with moisture, slick with moss, carved by centuries into shapes that almost suggest faces.

A wind moves through the branches above you, low and constant, like breathing.

## Into the Dark

Deeper in, where no light reaches the forest floor, the air changes. It carries the smell of cold water and wet earth, of something old and slow and utterly without urgency. The trees here do not lean — they loom.

You hear the stream before you see it. A thin sound, impossibly clear, cutting through the hush. You step around a boulder half-buried in leaf-rot and there it is: black water over white stones, moving fast despite the dark.

Across the stream, at the edge of a clearing, stands the well.

## The Well

It is older than it has any right to be. The stonework is not any style you know — the mortaring between the blocks has long since dissolved, and yet the structure holds, as if the stones have learned to need nothing but each other. Around the rim, carved into the lip in a spiral that goes all the way around, is a text you cannot read.

You lean over the edge. The water is far below, but you can see it — a faint glimmer, light from no clear source, silver and distant as a star.

Something looks back.

## After

You do not remember walking back. You are at the treeline, the valley spread below you in thin morning light, the smoke from the first fires beginning to rise from the rooftops.

You do not remember what you saw. You remember only the cold, and the sound of the well's surface breaking, and the way the silence felt afterward — not empty, but full of something you have no word for.

You do not go back.

But you do not forget, either.
